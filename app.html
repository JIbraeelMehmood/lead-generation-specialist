The best ways to find website clients

1. Target the right businesses

The best prospects are established local service businesses with no website or a bad one: plumbers, roofers, electricians, dentists, salons, auto repair shops, cleaners, landscapers and restaurants. They need a steady flow of calls, and each new customer is worth a lot to them. Look for 20+ Google reviews and a 4+ rating: those owners are busy and have money, but lose customers who search online.

2. Sell a "digital identity", not just a website

Owners buy outcomes: being found on Google, looking trustworthy, and getting bookings. Use the Digital identity check to show their score, what's missing and what competitors have. Then offer three packages (Starter, Growth, Premium). Most owners choose the middle one, and the monthly care plan gives you recurring income.

Only pitch an app to businesses with repeat customers (restaurants, gyms, salons, clinics, schools). For them, an installable web app (PWA) is far cheaper than a store app and installs from their website. For plumbers or lawyers, a great website with click-to-call is the right answer.

2b. Pitch something specific

Before contacting anyone, do 5 minutes of research with the Research buttons. Check their Google listing, Facebook and Instagram. Then name a real problem: "When I search 'plumber in Leeds' you don't show up with a site, but your 4.8-star competitor does." For weak sites, use the issues LeadFinder finds, such as not mobile-friendly, no HTTPS or slow loading.

3. Show, don't tell (the highest-converting method)

Build a quick demo homepage using their name, photos and reviews, then send them the link. Owners reply far more often to "I made this for you, take a look" than to a generic offer. Even a one-page template you customize in 30 minutes works.

4. Channel by country

  • USA: Phone calls and email work well. Call Tuesday to Thursday, mid-morning. Follow up by email with the demo link. Don't use auto-dialers or pre-recorded messages (TCPA rules), and honor "don't call me again".
  • UK: Business email to company addresses is generally allowed with a clear opt-out. Sole traders are treated more like consumers, so be careful. Check numbers against the TPS/CTPS do-not-call lists before cold calling.
  • Pakistan, India, UAE, Middle East: WhatsApp gets the most replies. Send a short voice note or message plus the demo link. Visiting in person also works very well.
  • Everywhere: Instagram and Facebook DMs work for salons, cafes, bakeries and boutiques that are active there.

This isn't legal advice. Rules differ by country, so check the local rules on cold calling and email before running a campaign.

5. Follow up (most deals happen here)

Most replies come after the 2nd to 4th contact. Use the Follow-up date in My list: contact on day 1, follow up on day 3, again on day 7 and a last time on day 14. Keep it short and friendly each time.

6. Other ways to get clients

  • Freelance sites: Upwork, Fiverr, Freelancer and PeoplePerHour. Build reviews there first.
  • Local Facebook groups and community groups where business owners ask for recommendations.
  • Partnerships with people who serve the same clients: printers, sign makers, accountants and marketing agencies.
  • Referrals: offer every happy client a discount or cash for each business they send you.
  • Your own portfolio site and a Google Business Profile for your web design service.

How to get a Google Places API key

If a search fails, press "Test Google key". The message tells you exactly which step to fix.

  1. Go to console.cloud.google.com and create a project.
  2. Turn on billing (Google requires a card; there is a monthly free usage allowance, so check their current pricing).
  3. Open "APIs & Services", then "Library", and enable Places API (New).
  4. Open "Credentials", create an API key. Under "API restrictions" choose Places API (New). Leave "Application restrictions" as None.
  5. Paste it above. The key stays on your computer.

Tip: set a daily quota limit in Google Cloud so you never get a surprise bill.
